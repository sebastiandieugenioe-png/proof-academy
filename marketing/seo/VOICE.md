# Sebastián's voice (rule for every guide, from 25/11/2026)

Sebastián doesn't talk like a magazine. The guides have to sound like him: a bar manager explaining something to a mate at the end of a shift. Learned from how he writes in chat: short, direct, no fluff, gets to the point, says "do this", doesn't dress things up.

## Do
- Short sentences. Lots of them. Some fragments are fine.
- Plain words a bartender uses on shift: "pour", "guest", "customer", "easy to drink", "goes down easy", "a bit of smoke".
- Contractions always: you'll, don't, it's, that's, I've.
- Talk straight to the reader: "you", "your shift", "your bar".
- Say what to do, then why in one line. "Ask where they're from. Takes two seconds and tells you loads."
- A bit of humour and honesty: "Nobody wants a lecture at 11pm on a Saturday."
- Real bar situations: busy Friday, the guest who "just wants a whiskey", the new guy on his first week.
- First person when it's his experience: "I always ask…", "What I do is…".
- Headings that sound spoken: "Don't just pour. Ask first." not "Recommended approach".

## Don't
- No posh or corporate words: approachable, transaction, elevate, curate, nuanced, delve, journey, crucial, infuse, signature style, "it's worth noting", "in conclusion", "whether you're a… or a…".
- No long sentences with three commas and a semicolon.
- No lecturing tone or textbook phrasing ("is known for", "is characterised by"). Say it like you'd say it at the bar.
- No forced slang or local Irish slang the international audience won't get. Casual, not cringe.
- No exaggeration or hype ("ultimate", "game-changer", "secret").

## Still true
- Facts only from the lessons, and they stay accurate. Casual tone never changes a fact.
- FAQ answers: short and casual too, but complete (Google shows them).
- English, international audience, legal drinking age, "Enjoy responsibly" stays.
