## r04_cocktail_balance.mp3
Reel 04 — Guide: cocktail recommendation. Sweet, sour or bitter + two options.

```
Still not sure what cocktail to make them?
Every good cocktail balances four things.
Sweet. Sour. Strong: that's the spirit. And weak: the water from the ice, or the mixer.
So ask: do you like it sweet, fresh and sour, or a bit bitter?
Then give them two options and let them pick. If they choose it, they'll enjoy it more.
```

```
Just a blonde, please.
Most guests don't know if they want a lager or an ale. Ask this.
Something fresh, or something with more flavour?
Fresh? A crisp lager, like a pilsner. Served cold, three to seven degrees.
More flavour? An IPA, but warn them: it's bitter. A wheat beer, with banana and clove. Or even a stout.
```

## r06_lager_not_colour.mp3
Reel 06 — Guide: ale vs lager. It's the yeast, not the colour.

```
Lager just means light beer, right? Nope.
It's not the colour. It's the yeast, and the temperature.
Lager ferments cold, around seven to thirteen degrees: clean and crisp. Ale ferments warm, and that gives fruity, spicy notes.
Bonus: lager comes from the German lagern, to store. And most beer drunk in the world is lager.
```

## r10_navy_strength.mp3
Reel 10 — Guide: drink stories. Navy Strength gin and the gunpowder.

```
Why is it called Navy Strength gin?
It's around fifty-seven percent or more.
The story: gunpowder soaked in spirit that strong would still light. That was the proof the booze wasn't watered down.
Part of it is probably legend, so tell it with a wink.
```
